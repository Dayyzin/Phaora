# Chapter I · A seat is kept for you

Draft, 14 Nov 2026. The book retold as a poem (David: "a poem that tells the story ... introduces all the cast, but not
deliberately"). Same plot and ending as the film and the book. The story is free; every spec named is true.

---

**Prologue**

There is a cinema in snow
that plays one reel on Christmas Eve.
No tickets sold, no one to show
the way, and no one wants to leave.

The front row's full but for a chair,
a little card upon its seat:
no name, one mark, and waiting there
for someone coming down the street.

**I. Snow**

It snowed since four; the bulbs burned gold,
the street had gone to hush and white,
when down it came, ignoring cold,
a coat that took its time tonight.

Gelosia: black lamb, cut thin,
a net of diamonds, nine by seven,
so cream silk glows from deep within
like porch lights left on low in heaven.

Eight buttons, belt, a strap each cuff,
a hem that swung to mid-calf, black;
the snow tried hard, but not enough:
it settled, sparkled, and slid back.

**II. The doors**

One wreath upon the brass, no more,
to say what night the night might be;
the coat walked up, and then the door
swung open wide without a key.

Inside, the warm. Behind, the snow.
A single mark, cut through to cream,
low on its back began to glow
the way things do inside a dream.

**III. The aisle**

Inside, the reel was running; three,
the screen began, and counted down.
The coat came quicker than need be,
the only hurry in the town.

At two, the light came up to meet it.
Ödara hung across a seat:
black lamb in five, a face (don't greet it,
it's made of holes) in cream, complete.

Ritornelo lay along the arm,
two long black gloves, one seam, brass snap,
left fingers-together, calm,
the way you'd say, *I'm coming back.*

**IV. The front row**

The front row: every seat was taken,
though nobody had bought a chair.
Coema, handles down, unshaken,
black calf, brass zip, sat quiet there.

Voluta, round as any reel,
at twenty-two by seven deep,
wore sixteen squares around its wheel,
the kind of tin a film would keep.

Across the aisle, Itaoca sat,
one handle lowered, brass disc bright;
then Coaraci, coiled up flat,
its disc turned, like a face, to light.

They sat as if they'd seen the show
and loved it, and would see it twice:
still, faces to the screen's warm glow,
and wanting nothing told. How nice.

**V. The kept seat**

The count went three, then two, then stopped.
The screen would never say the one,
for third along, one seat was kept:
a card, a mark, a place for one.

An O, two dots, a line drawn through,
no name; it didn't need one, quite.
The coat sat down, the way coats do,
all at once, then settled right.

The leather sighed; the sleeves found arms;
eight buttons caught the light to stay,
and held it close, like little charms
you keep, and never give away.

**VI. Reel one**

Then on the screen, a picture, new,
so new it hadn't happened yet:
a lobby, curtain tied back, through
to Heliodora's silhouette,

a black hoodie, its back to the room,
a cream kiss knitted, dot by dot;
a café, Cordel's silk in bloom,
knotted by a second cup, still hot.

Under the marquee, a car,
its passenger door left open wide;
Öfusca, dark glasses, folded, far
from done with night, on the dash inside.

And at the box office, one fare:
Öbolo, black, a disc of gold,
a card case left for someone there,
the only ticket ever sold.

Then the street in snow, tonight's own snow;
and in the front row, through the net,
the screen's light found the silk below,
the warmest thing the night had met.

**VII. Reel two**

A suite by day: the vanity,
its stool turned out to face the door;
Cantilena, squared in P, Ö, P,
black, cream, and cream on cream once more.

And on the glass lay Lustre, strung
in crystal and in gold, and hung
a disc, a diamond at its heart,
that caught the lamp and played its part.

Then midnight: one lamp left alight,
the bed turned down on just one side;
Alvor, cream silk, a slip so light,
hung from the wardrobe, open wide.

And on the wall, in plain gold, rose
Öitava, crescents, spindle through,
a star inside that only shows
when someone's looking. Maybe you.

The screen went pale; down in the row
eight buttons paled along with it.
Last: morning, bench, a window's glow,
one rail, one hanger. Nothing on it yet.

**VIII. House lights**

The house lights rose, too soon, as they do.
The row sat just the way it had:
Coema's handles down; the gloves laid too;
Coaraci's disc to the white screen, glad.

Not one had moved. That's what they do,
the good films: leave you as you were.
But where the coat had sat, a new
small thing sat waiting in its stead:

no card. A box, black, square, and on
its lid, a disc of brass, round as the one
the screen would never say. And gone
the coat, as if the night were done.

**IX. The morning**

Then Christmas morning, in the suite
the screen had shown the night before:
the coat hung on the rail, complete,
a small fir green beside the door.

And on the bench, the box, undone:
black knit, in tissue, folded through,
and on it, still, the card. Just one
small mark. Still no name. That's for you.

The seat was kept for the coat, it's true.
The coat was kept, all along, for you.

**Coda**

Up in the booth, reel one came free,
another threaded, wound and spun;
and on the screen, for none to see,
in red, a II, where there'd been one.

*Thank you for the pleasure of your company.*

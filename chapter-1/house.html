PHAÖRA

the house

PHAÖRA is a house of knitwear, leather and silk, in black, cream and gold. Chapter I is its first line: fifteen pieces, each one a stitch chart, a cut plan or a print file before it is anything else.

the mark

An O, two dots above it, a line drawn through. It is on the card that keeps the seat, on every clasp and disc, and inside every piece.

the names

Each piece is named from Minas Gerais and Brazil: its gold, its baroque, its old words.

  • GelosiaThe Veil CoatThe carved lattice screen on colonial houses, which a woman could see through without being seen. The coat’s net is that lattice.
  • ÖdaraThe Muse Bomber“Beautiful, at peace”: a Yoruba word that passed into Brazilian speech.
  • AlvorThe silk slipA literary word for the first whiteness of dawn.
  • HeliodoraThe MuseBárbara Heliodora, the poet-heroine of the 1789 Minas conspiracy; also heliodor, the golden gem of Minas.
  • CantilenaThe PÖ hoodieA song that keeps returning to the same few notes: the PÖ said again and again.
  • VolutaThe ReelThe spiral scroll carved on the baroque churches of Minas, and a coil like a reel of film.
  • CoemaThe MatineeOld Tupi for morning.
  • ItaocaThe PÖ top-handleOld Tupi: itá stone and oka house, “house of stone”.
  • RitorneloThe opera glovesThe refrain that comes back, in opera: the gloves are left to say someone is coming back.
  • CoaraciThe disc beltOld Tupi for the sun.
  • ÖboloThe card holderThe small coin paid for passage.
  • CordelThe silk scarfBrazil’s string literature: black-and-cream woodcut booklets hung in a row on a cord, like the scarf’s frames.
  • ÖfuscaThe sunglasses“She dazzles”, and also “she darkens”.

the story

One night, told the same way everywhere: the film, the book, the shorts and the register tell one story, "A Seat Is Kept for You".

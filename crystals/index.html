01/The birth of a geode

A Room That
Filled Up

Lava cools from the outside in. Gas trapped inside has nowhere to go, and what it leaves behind is a cavity — a void in solid rock, closed, empty, and in no hurry at all.

Then water finds it, carrying dissolved silica and moving so slowly that moving is a generous word for it.

02/The purple

Colour Is Damage

Iron came in with the water, and the ground around it was radioactive enough to do the rest. That is all amethyst is — quartz with iron in it, that sat in the dark long enough for the dark to colour it.

The deepest purple sits at the tip, where the crystal grew last and slowest. Split one open and you can read its own calendar off the wall: pale where it started, saturated where it stopped.

04/Why a mason

The Same Eye,
Different Magnification

A mason spends his working life arguing with rock about where it wants to break. The bed. The grain. The face that will take a chisel, and the face that will shatter if you ask it to.

After enough years you can look at a stone on a pallet and know, before you touch it, which way it opens. That skill has no idea what scale it is working at. Point it at a two-ton step and it sets a step. Point it at a geode and it does exactly the same thing.

05/The light

Most Stone
Stops Light

A wall is a thing light does not get through, which is why a mason thinks in shadow, in reveal, in what a chamfer does with a low sun in October.

These do the opposite. Light goes into them and comes back out somewhere else, and the carving has to be planned around it. Cut a wing too thin and it glows. Cut it too thick and it goes dead.

Hardness

Quartz is a seven. Fluorite is a four. The number decides how fine the cut can get.

Cleavage

Where the stone has already decided it will break, whatever the carver wants.

Light

How deep it travels before it comes back out. It is what makes a wing glow.

06/Knowing when

Already Finished

Fluorite cleaves perfectly in four directions at once, so there is no forgiving pass — one stroke with too much weight behind it takes a corner off, and there is no putting it back. It is why so little of it is ever carved into anything.

This one was not carved. It was lit, set on a stand, and left exactly as it came out of the ground. Someone looked at it and decided that. It is the harder decision.

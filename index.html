Fieldstone Wall · Driveway

Field notes

I
Below the frost line
New England ground moves in winter. A footing poured above the frost line lifts, and the wall on top of it cracks. Ours go below it — every time, no exception for budget.
II
Cure before load
Mortar hits handling strength in days but keeps gaining for weeks after. We don't load a wall or open a walkway early just to hit a schedule.
III
Tooled, not troweled flat
A concave joint sheds water off the face of the stone. A flush one holds it there. It costs seconds with a jointer, and it's why the wall stays dry.
IV
Give the water somewhere to go
A retaining wall holds back soil, not water. Crushed stone behind the face and a drain pipe at the base give the water an exit — without one, it builds up and pushes the wall out.
V
The base does the work
A patio moves if what's under it does. Compacted subgrade, a compacted gravel base, then a thin bedding layer — the pavers on top are the easy part.
VI
Every tread has a pitch
A dead-flat step holds water, and in a New England winter that means ice. Every tread we set carries a slight forward pitch so water runs off instead of sitting.

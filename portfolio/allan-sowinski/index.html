The Whole Job, One Frame
What Was There

Nothing here had failed outright. It had just been moving for years — steps settling at different rates, joints opening, the path narrowing every season as the beds grew into it. That's the point at which most people stop noticing, and start walking around it instead.

How It Got There6 steps
Step 01 · The base

Concrete first, stone second

The old walk moved because there was nothing under it. This one starts with a poured, cured slab — and the flag gets sorted and staged around it before a single piece is committed.

Step 02 · The bed

Full mortar, every stone

Each piece goes down in a full bed, not spotted on five dabs and levelled by eye. Nothing is floated on sand and hoped over — that's exactly what was taken out of here.

Step 03 · Line and level

The string goes down first

Pulled before the first piece lands, and every stone after it checked against it. Height, pitch and run all come off that string. Get it wrong here and no amount of good stonework hides it.

Step 04 · Cut to fit

No pattern to fall back on

Irregular flag has no repeat. Each stone is picked and shaped to meet the ones already down, so the joints stay tight instead of turning into a field of mortar with rocks in it.

Step 05 · The edge

Cobble both sides, cut by hand

Granite cobble set down each side, then the bed cut back to it. That edge is the reason this walk won't disappear under the plantings the way the last one did.

Step 06 · Steps

Full blocks, four risers

Solid granite instead of small cobbles stacked into a rise, carried on rebuilt wall either side. Every riser the same height, so nobody arriving at this house has to think about their feet.

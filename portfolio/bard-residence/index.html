The Bard Residence

A wall, and the ground it holds.

A block retaining wall carrying the grade back off the house, granite treads set into the run, the whole side of the property, and the front entrance. Every stage as it went in, in the order it happened.

The walk
Before anything is priced

Before anything is priced, you walk the ground with the person who has to live on it. Where the water goes, what has to stay, what the grade is really doing behind the house.

The base
Base and first courses

Ties out, ground opened, base compacted in lifts. The first course is the one that matters — every course above it inherits whatever you got wrong here, so it gets checked, pulled and checked again before the second one goes on.

The wall
Wall up, treads in

Wall carried along the house line with the granite treads set into it as it goes, rather than cut in afterwards. Cap stones levelled one at a time. Backfill and the top of the grade come next.

Steps and edge
Steps, cap and the finished edge

Granite treads set into the wall, cap course carried back to the house, and the cobble edge that holds the bed line and moves the roof water off the foundation.

Front steps
Curved bluestone out, granite in

What was at the door was a curved bluestone flight on a thin stone skirt. What is there now is thermal-finish granite: full slabs, two risers deep, run out to a landing that reaches the sill. The skirt under each tread is laid course by course and pointed as it goes, and the joints between the slabs are sponged off while the mortar is still green — once it cures on the face it comes off with acid or not at all.

Under the deck
Fence panel closing off the storage nook

Same trip, different trade — the open space under the deck gets a matching vinyl fence panel and gate instead of staying open to the yard. Set plumb against the existing corner post before anything gets fastened down.

The side of the house
Walk, beds and the enclosure, finished

The whole side of the house is in. Washed stone laid between two cobble edges that curve with the wall rather than cutting straight past it, beds mulched and planted to the line, and stepping stones set through black river stone where the walk turns for the door. The vinyl enclosure is closed in and hung — gate plumb, latch set, the run finished down to the mulch line.

The front entrance
Granite steps and landing, at the doors

The last corner. Three granite treads the full width of the doors, each one a single piece with the rock face left on its edge, carried on courses of laid stone rather than a poured wall with a face stuck to it. The landing above is the same granite, cut square and jointed tight, and the bottom tread meets the new drive without a lip to catch a toe or a shovel.

Anyone can set the middle. The edge tells you who built it.
